# Daniel Locatelli: profile

## About me

I am a doctoral researcher at Gramazio Kohler Research, ETH Zurich. I research how timber plate structures are designed and built, and how AI changes the way architects and engineers work. The homepage has an AI chat where you can ask about my research, projects, and teaching, and a list of essays on what stays valuable in architecture and engineering once code is cheap.

Daniel Locatelli is Brazilian, originally from Vilhena, Rondonia, Brazil. He moved to Germany in 2019 to pursue his Master's degree at the University of Stuttgart. He later moved to Switzerland for his doctorate at ETH Zurich and is currently based in Zurich, Switzerland.

## Services I offer

### Agentic systems engineering

AI agents that work with your tools and data: MCP servers that give agents access to software and databases, agent skills that encode domain knowledge, and LLM-powered assistants grounded in your own content. Built for architecture, engineering and construction workflows.

### Plugin development

Bespoke tools to automate tasks, integrate specialized analyses, or extend a software capability. Expert in C# for Rhino/Grasshopper, Revit and AutoCAD.

### Web applications

Web-based solutions for project management, collaborative tools, financial simulators and 3D visualization. Focused on React, Astro and PostgreSQL.

### Computational design

Applying advanced computational design strategies to streamline and enhance AEC processes, improving efficiency in design, analysis, and documentation.

### Data visualization

Creating dashboards to better understand, interpret, and act upon complex project data. From web-native dashboards to enterprise Power BI frameworks.

### Digital fabrication

Creating Grasshopper scripts to automatically extract manufacturing data from complex, parametric 3D designs for digital fabrication.

### Building Across Scales
ITECH - University of Stuttgart | Stuttgart, Germany | December 2024 | /research/building-across-scales
Tags: Computational design, Form finding, Robotics
The research proposes a heterogeneous multi-scalar robotic construction system to further automate on-site timber construction. Specifically, it presents the next step in the automation of on-site gluing by introducing a custom clamping robot for the on-site pressuring of timber elements. Therefore, at the core of the research lies the development of the clamping robot as part of a larger robotic construction team, including an industrial robotic arm and an automated crane in co-design with the material and building system.
