  ### The claim

  ## The answer stops being a verdict
notes: "The result is the set of all designs that work. When a design is not possible: a named list of exactly which requirements exclude it. An engineer can trace which limit bound which decision. This is the slide for whoever signs: traceability, not a black box."
---

---
text: |
  ### What is a coupling?

  ## How the panel is made decides what it can carry

  *Press force. Glue area. Stock thickness.*
notes: "Master thesis, on-site gluing of timber panels: the structural checks took fabrication variables as inputs. Concrete before abstract: in the master thesis we called this a material-robotic system. Same material, same predicament as our plates."
---

---
text: |
  ### The vocabulary

  ## A coupling is *declared* when it travels as data
notes: "A shared variable both modules read: glue area, press force, stock thickness. Chip design rules are exactly this: written-down limits that every module respects. Declared means written down as data, nothing more mysterious."
---

---
image: entangledRig
imageAlt: "A one-off timber test piece buried under clamps, glue squeeze-out, wires, and improvised sensors on a workshop bench (AI-generated image)"
overlay: black/45
text: |
  ### The risk

  ## Some couplings may not travel as data
notes: "If pressing or gluing changes load-bearing in ways only tests can reveal, the modules collapse into one entangled model: a one-off, built for this system only. Honest failure mode. A one-off checker would still be worth building, but the broader claim would not survive it."
---

---
text: |
  ### The question for this room

  ## *Chips* declare their couplings.

  # Can we declare *ours*?
notes: "That is precisely what the test programme establishes. Where does the shared-variable picture break? Their fabrication and testing experience answers it, not my code."
---

---
text: |
  ### Why now

  ## When the design is code, LLMs excel at it
notes: "The idea is decades old; earlier attempts stayed prototypes, and every constraint was written and simplified by hand. What changed: constraint modules can now be drafted from test protocols and documentation, iterated in minutes. If asked for names: SpaceSolver at EPFL around 2000 documents the hand-written-inequalities problem; SEED in the 1990s is the other reference point. Do not attribute a cause of death to SEED; the record does not support one."
---

image: failureTest

text: |
  ### The guardrail

  ## AI cannot make a constraint true. Only the tests can.
notes: "Limit values and their uncertainty come from one place only: the physical tests. Every drafted constraint is checked against them before it counts. The work shifts from writing to reviewing. The test programme is the ground truth that makes any of the AI usable."
---

text: |
  ### The SNF project

  ## *compas_timber* is already building the paths
notes: "The connection to fabrication exists today; connecting structural analysis is the goal. Together they are already enough of a playground to test this hypothesis. Instrument, not subject: the thesis is the claim about declared couplings and the admissible region; compas_timber is where it runs."
