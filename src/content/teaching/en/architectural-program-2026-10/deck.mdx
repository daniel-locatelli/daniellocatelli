<Slide>
  <div class="pp-head">
    <h3>The question</h3>
    <h2>What does an agentic workflow look like for a <em>real architectural design study?</em></h2>
  </div>
  <div class="ap-body"><FlowPipeline /></div>
  <p class="pp-foot">Every run is isolated: a fresh container on its own network, no memory, skills or CLAUDE.md, no name in any input, and the API token held by a proxy outside the agent.</p>
  <SlideNotes>The agent gets what an architect gets at the start of a study and has 60 minutes to deliver an IFC. Every design is then scored, rendered and logged the same way.</SlideNotes>
</Slide>

<Slide>
  <div class="pp-head">
    <h3>Finding 1</h3>
    <h2>The program is <em>already a prompt</em></h2>
  </div>
  <div class="ap-body">
    <div class="ap-cols">
      <div class="ap-doc">
        <span class="ap-doc-name">program.md · the architect's brief</span>
        <p><b>Task.</b> Design the enclosure of the extension: roof and walls. The interior stays free of supports.</p>
        <p><b>Use.</b> Classrooms and presentation halls for university teaching.</p>
        <p><b>Constraints.</b> Inside the envelope. The roof bears only at the support points at the cores, within 0.005 m. Clear height at least 3.0 m. Planar CLT plates, 140 mm.</p>
        <p><b>Documents.</b> existing.ifc, limits.ifc, site plan, photos.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="ap-doc">
        <span class="ap-doc-name">task.md · what the agent receives</span>
        <p>You are the designer of a roof structure for an existing building. The program, the 3D model of the existing building, the site, photos and drawings are in /work/brief/. Read all of it before you start.</p>
        <p>Deliver, in /work/out/: design.ifc, references.md, statement.md.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
  <p class="pp-foot">Plain text: what the building should have, its constraints, its site. The design process converges on writing the program.</p>
  <SlideNotes>A program states what, where and under which constraints, in plain text: the shape of a prompt. The task text is short; the program and the two IFC files carry the rest.</SlideNotes>
</Slide>

    <h3>The experiment</h3>
    <h2>Five variables, one changed at a time, <em>ten repeats each</em></h2>
  </div>
  <div class="ap-body">
    <div class="ap-factors">
      <div><b>Task</b><span><i>P</i> plate roof, glass facade</span><span><i>H</i> P, roof of hyperbolic paraboloids</span><span><i>F</i> H, plus BTLx for the CNC</span></div>
      <div><b>Tools</b><span><i>py</i> Python, numpy, ifcopenshell</span><span><i>compas</i> the COMPAS ecosystem</span><span><i>blender</i> Blender with ifcopenshell</span></div>
      <div><b>Context</b><span><i>C0</i> the task text</span><span><i>C1</i> + rules.md: every check, every threshold</span><span><i>C2</i> + 19 papers on meshes</span><span><i>C3</i> + a skills library</span></div>
      <div><b>Workflow</b><span><i>W0</i> one designer, 60 min</span><span><i>W1</i> designer, three reviewers, revision</span></div>
      <div><b>Harness</b><span><i>cc</i> Claude Code</span><span><i>codex</i> Codex (blocked)</span></div>
    </div>
  </div>
  <p class="pp-foot">A run id reads task, tools, context, workflow, harness, repeat: <code>H-compas-C0-W0-cc-r01</code>. Baseline: H-compas-C0-W0-cc.</p>
  <SlideNotes>Five variables, 144 cells, 10 repeats each. Tier 1 changes one variable at a time against the baseline; the model is the same throughout. The next five slides show each variable.</SlideNotes>
</Slide>

<Slide>
  <div class="pp-head">

    <h2>Each task level adds <em>one sentence</em> to the same prompt</h2>
  </div>
  <div class="ap-body">
    <div class="ap-cols">
      <div class="ap-doc ap-prompt">
        <span class="ap-doc-name">task.md · the passage that changes</span>
        <p>Design the enclosure of the extension: its roof, made of planar cross-laminated timber (CLT) plates, 140 mm thick, and its walls, a glass curtain-wall facade.<mark>form</mark> You may combine the plates with beams. The interior must stay free of supports. […] The envelope, support points and wall zone are in limits.ifc.<mark>fabrication</mark></p>
      </div>
      <dl class="ap-levels">
        <div><dt>P</dt><dd><i>nothing added</i> A plate roof and a glass facade.</dd></div>
        <div><dt>H</dt><dd><b>form:</b> "The roof is formed of one or more hyperbolic paraboloids (hypars), each built from planar plates."</dd></div>
        <div><dt>F</dt><dd><b>form</b> as H, and <b>fabrication:</b> "The roof must be fabricable: deliver also design.btlx, a valid BTLx file with one part per roof plate, as a timber CNC would receive it."</dd></div>
      </dl>
    </div>
  </div>
  <SlideNotes>Every variable works the same way: one passage of a fixed task text. The task levels add the hypar form (H) and the BTLx delivery (F).</SlideNotes>
</Slide>

<Slide>
  <div class="pp-head">
    <h3>Variable 4 · Workflow</h3>
    <h2>One designer, or a designer with <em>three reviewers</em></h2>
  </div>
  <div class="ap-body"><FlowWorkflow /></div>
  <p class="pp-foot">Reviewers get the same brief and context level, read the design, and may not change it. In W1 the draft is scored too, so the effect of the review shows within a run.</p>
  <SlideNotes>W1 adds three parallel reviews (compliance, structure and fabrication, architecture) and a 20-minute revision in the designer's own session.</SlideNotes>
</Slide>

<Slide>
  <div class="pp-head">
    <h3>Tools · Blender, 64 runs</h3>
    <h2>Blender is used as a <em>Python interpreter</em></h2>
  </div>
  <div class="ap-body"><RouteBlender /></div>
  <p class="pp-foot">Not one call to the Blender API, and no MCP server in any harness. Counts overlap: 33 runs used both routes.</p>
  <SlideNotes>No bpy call in 64 runs. Blender's bundled Python runs the same numpy and ifcopenshell script as everywhere else.</SlideNotes>

<Slide>
  <div class="pp-head">
    <h3>Tools · COMPAS, 94 runs</h3>
    <h2>COMPAS is used as a <em>vector library</em>, or not at all</h2>
  </div>
  <div class="ap-body"><RouteCompas /></div>
  <p class="pp-foot">compas_ifc is installed and never used. In task F the plate script hands the same plates to compas_timber: in 2 runs within the script, in 8 through a plate file it writes for a second script.</p>
  <SlideNotes>COMPAS supplies vectors at most. One script computes the plates and writes the IFC; the BTLx comes from the same plates, never from reading the IFC back.</SlideNotes>
</Slide>

<Slide>
  <div class="pp-head">
    <h3>Tools · Python, 65 runs</h3>
    <h2>Every stack ends in the <em>same script</em></h2>
  </div>
  <div class="ap-body"><RoutePy /></div>
  <p class="pp-foot">The design medium is the same in all three stacks: numpy computes the geometry, ifcopenshell writes the IFC entity by entity. The host and the framework are bypassed.</p>
  <SlideNotes>36 runs reached for scipy or trimesh and hit an import error: 7 installed them, 29 wrote the step in numpy. The tools variable changes which libraries import, not how the agent designs.</SlideNotes>

<Slide>
  <div class="pp-head">
    <h3>Finding 2</h3>
    <h2>The designer now defines <em>what "done" means</em></h2>
  </div>
  <div class="ap-body">
    <div class="ap-cols ap-cols-wide">
      <div class="ap-rules">
        <div><span class="ap-ico is-ifc" data-ext="ifc"></span><b>limits.ifc</b><span>Envelope, support points, wall zone: containment in a solid</span></div>
        <div><span class="ap-ico" data-ext="md"></span><b>bearing.md</b><span>Snap to the given support points, within 5 mm</span></div>
        <div><span class="ap-ico" data-ext="md"></span><b>coverage.md</b><span>95 % of the roof in plan; plates within 3.0 × 13.0 m</span></div>
        <div><span class="ap-ico" data-ext="md"></span><b>connectivity.md</b><span>A graph to a bearing; a plane fit per plate, 1 mm</span></div>
        <div><span class="ap-ico is-btlx" data-ext="md"></span><b>material.md</b><span>CLT 140 mm and its properties; BTLx for the timber CNC <i>not checked yet</i></span></div>
        <div><span class="ap-ico" data-ext="md"></span><b>hypar.md</b><span>90 % of plates on a hypar within 10 mm, at most 4 per corner</span></div>
        <p class="ap-rules-axis">simple checks<i></i>complex algorithms</p>
      </div>
      <div class="ap-doc">
        <span class="ap-doc-name">Who does what</span>
        <p><b>The architect</b> writes the program and the rules, from simple checks to complex algorithms.</p>
        <p><b>The agent</b> does the modelling: every plate, every IFC entity, every line of geometry code.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
  <SlideNotes>The rules are files the architect writes: text, and IFC where geometry is the rule. Handing them to the agent was the largest single improvement. Material is not checked yet; the next slide shows what that costs.</SlideNotes>
</Slide>

    <h2>What no rule checks <em>comes out thin</em></h2>
  </div>
  <div class="ap-body">
    <div class="ap-cols">
      <div class="ap-bars" style="--of:221">
        <span class="ap-doc-name">The checker tests geometry only · beyond it, designs carrying each, of 221</span>
        <div><b>A material</b><i class="is-low" style="--n:150"></i><span>150</span></div>
        <div><b>Property sets</b><i class="is-low" style="--n:91"></i><span>91</span></div>
        <div><b>Surface styles</b><i class="is-low" style="--n:3"></i><span>3</span></div>
        <div><b>Quantity sets</b><i class="is-low" style="--n:0"></i><span>0</span></div>
        <p class="ap-bars-note">The brief fixes 140 mm CLT; 71 designs name no material at all.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="ap-doc">
        <span class="ap-doc-name">BTLx · task F, 10 runs, no rules.md</span>
        <p><b>Relabelled, not validated.</b> compas_timber writes version 2.0.0; all 10 runs replaced the string with 2.3.1, and only 4 checked it against the schema.</p>
        <p><b>Valid is not cuttable.</b> Every part is one square-cut contour: no bevel at the folds, no joints, slivers down to 0.083 mm.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
  <p class="pp-foot">The IFC arrives as a geometry container, the BTLx as a relabelled file. Defining done has to cover material and fabrication too.</p>
  <SlideNotes>The checker only tests geometry. Whatever it does not test (material, properties, quantities, a valid and cuttable BTLx) came out thin. Task F ran only at C0, so its agents never saw the BTLx rule.</SlideNotes>
</Slide>

{/* ───────────────────────────── Outlook ───────────────────────────── */}

<Slide>
  <div class="pp-head">
    <h3>Hypothesis · to validate</h3>
    <h2>If the rules are the design, <em>make checking them instant</em></h2>
  </div>
  <div class="ap-body">
    <div class="ap-cols">
      <pre class="ap-code"><code>{`panel = TimberPanel(length=4.8)
roof  = Hypar(panels, supports=limits.points)

$ check roof
error    bearing: plate 12 is 31 mm off S2
warning  plate 7: span near the CLT limit
ok       planarity, envelope, coverage`}</code></pre>
      <div class="ap-doc">
        <span class="ap-doc-name">A domain-specific language</span>
        <p>The model is written in the language and the checks live <b>inside</b> it, like Lean's type checker, so the agent gets feedback from a plain command line.</p>
        <p>Severities like TypeScript's: <b>errors, warnings, suggestions</b>. Measured rules with variance map onto that gradient.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
  <p class="pp-foot">Illustrative syntax. Whether this beats checking the IFC after the fact is the open question.</p>
  <SlideNotes>A hypothesis, not a result: if the rules are the design, checking them should be instant and inside the modelling language, as Lean checks proofs. To validate against today's IFC-plus-checker loop.</SlideNotes>
</Slide>
