notes: "Stuttgart. Engineered timber is glued in a factory because gluing needs pressure, and pressure needs a press. That is why elements are limited by what a truck carries, and why timber buildings end up on a grid. With Nils Opgenorth I asked what happens if the press goes to the site. A crane, an industrial arm and a custom clamping robot as one team. Every certainty a factory gives you is gone. You will spend six afternoons with an ABB arm doing exactly this."

  ### What I work on now

notes: "Zurich. Timber plate building systems, in the SNSF project CEDD. Plates joined along their narrow side edges. Eurocode 5 does not cover side-by-side gluing of plates, so every configuration needs physical tests and a special approval before anyone may build it. Note what is certain and what is not. The material is new, so I know the stock, the sizes, the grade. What I do not know is how the joint behaves. My uncertainty is not in the wood. It is in the connection, and in the rules that stop before it."

title: An AI harness for AEC
image: aiHarness
imageAlt: "One shared model sits at the centre of a panel labelled the AI harness. Design intent enters from above and lands on the model, the harness reaches out to five checks ringed around it, structure, fabricability, material, cost and carbon, and each returns a dashed line that passes back through the harness into the model."
fit: contain
notes: "So here is the thesis. Checking a building design takes weeks, so it happens once or twice per project, and design stops at the first version that passes. Put the agent inside a harness instead: it proposes, and structure, fabricability, material, cost and carbon check continuously, each against the code that governs it, every verdict written back to one model. Make checking nearly free and you can explore. Your uncertainty this week is in the stock, an offcut's shape unknown until you hold it. Mine is in the joint. Different unknown, same closed loop, and that is what Wednesday is about. Come argue with me."
